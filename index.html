Floor vs. ceiling

consistent scorers vs. streaky, high-upside ones

Two players can carry the same average and still feel completely different on your bench. A ▮▮▮ steady tag means the production shows up almost every night — usually a stable, high-touch role or a game built on defense/rebounding rather than makes-dependent scoring. A ▮▮▮ steady, 50+ FP/gm tag is the same reliability but at a true stud scoring level — these are the safest high-value picks on the whole board. A ⚡ boom-bust tag means real week-to-week swings — often a 3-point-heavy shot diet, a matchup-dependent role, or injury/minutes variance — paired with a higher ceiling on the good nights. This is a qualitative read on shot profile and role, not a variance calculation from game logs, so use it to decide who you can count on for a stable week vs. who you're rostering for spike weeks.

5-year trajectory

rewarding players still climbing, discounting ones on the back nine

Beyond last season's numbers, everything on this board also reflects where a player has been trending over roughly the last five years. A RISING tag (young player still adding to their game year over year) gets a modest boost to where they land; a DECLINING tag (falling games played, fading efficiency, or a clear age/health cliff over multiple seasons) gets pulled down, stacking on top of the injury-risk discount where both apply. No tag just means "still performing at a stable level" — not every established star is trending anywhere in particular, and that's fine. This is a basketball-knowledge judgment call, not a regression on five years of game logs, so treat it the same way as the other tags: a nudge, not a verdict.
